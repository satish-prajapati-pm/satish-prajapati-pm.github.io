The challenge

As a Shopify app, the product's entire addressable market was one platform's merchants — and every capability it could offer was bounded by that platform.

The objective

Make the product independent: one standalone application that merchants integrate into Shopify, WooCommerce or Etsy — and eventually use with no store at all.

My role

Delivery lead from the original Shopify app through the standalone transition and into the current build — scope and roadmap, integration planning across three commerce platforms, team coordination, QA and release.

Key decision

Problem

Growth was capped by the host platform, and every new channel meant rebuilding the same product again.

Options

A — Stay a Shopify app
B — Separate apps per platform
C — One standalone product with integrations

Decision

C — Standalone core, thin integration layer per platform.

Reason

One product to build and support, three markets to sell into — and a foundation that could later serve merchants with no store at all.

Delivered

A standalone application live with Shopify, WooCommerce and Etsy integrations, with project management for the standalone product established and running.

In progress

Custom order management so merchants without an e-commerce store can sell directly through the product.

[MERCHANT / ADOPTION METRIC TO BE PROVIDED]

Lesson

Going platform-independent is a product decision long before it's an engineering one. Get the core domain right and each new channel becomes an integration, not a rewrite.

08 / About

Satish Prajapati.

I've spent seven years in the space between a business goal and a shipped product — where requirements are still vague, three teams disagree, and someone has to make the call.

My approach is straightforward: clarity over chaos. Structure the work, make dependencies and risks visible early, keep stakeholders in context rather than in status meetings, and hold the team to outcomes rather than output. Whether it's a 42-person cross-functional team or a solo AI platform launch, the job is the same — remove ambiguity so people can do their best work.

The problems I enjoy most are the tangled ones: systems that have to talk to each other, vendors on different timelines, a legacy platform that can't be switched off, a launch date that already exists. I have a computer-engineering background, so I can sit in a technical discussion and understand the trade-off being made — which is usually where delivery risk actually lives.

Based in Ahmedabad, India — working with clients across Asia, the Middle East, Europe and the US.
